# Backyard Astronaut — Unified Spatial Reconstruction V2

Status: approved direction / implementation specification. This document does not certify an implemented or tested engine.
Supersedes the PixiJS-first rendering architecture and mode-separated Alpha 0.3 design. Retains the full approved gameplay scope. No Vercel. Primary delivery: GitHub Pages catalog and direct playable URL; standalone desktop and in-chat companion remain scope items.

## Non-negotiable experience
The player walks through a detailed rotatable neighborhood, finds physical salvage, carries it to a real workbench, attaches it to an actual spacecraft, boards that craft, launches it from the yard, flies through the atmosphere, establishes orbit, navigates to other bodies and returns or lands. The same world state, stable entity IDs, installed part transforms, damage, fuel and ownership persist through every transition. Menus inspect or manipulate the world, not replace it. No pre-authored flight path or fake orbit success. Creative mode and hybrid discovery progression. No arbitrary menu-driven teleportation between gameplay stages.

## Stack and deployment
Three.js WebGL2, TypeScript, Vite; Rapier 3D WASM for local contact and structural interaction; typed Float64 orbital state and numerical propagation, Web Workers for trajectory previews; Web Audio spatial audio; IndexedDB versioned saves with import/export. GPU-instanced raster stacks and pixel-textured meshes, original or appropriately licensed assets only, asset manifest with license/source/scale. Pin audited dependency versions in lockfile, bundle them locally; no CDN-only production dependency. Build using relative base path for GitHub Pages. Maintain existing catalog and other games. Desktop package from same build via Electron or equivalent after compatibility testing. Companion in-chat version is derivative, never the primary implementation. If WebGL2 or WASM fails, show a clear compatibility error and retain previous verified build; do not replace the engine with a software drawing diagnostic.

## Rendering and assets
One render pipeline and coherent materials across terrain, character, salvage, tools, workshop, craft, launch and space. Each gameplay-relevant item is an independent reusable asset and entity. Local horizontal RGBA cross-sections in model coordinates, depth writes, alpha-tested cutouts, consistent texel scale, pivot and attachment sockets. Instance repeated slices/props by atlas and material; provide side faces or voxel-surface meshes where thin slices reveal gaps at grazing angles. Use 3D textured surfaces for terrain, planets, clouds and spherical bodies; do not force enormous planets into stacks of translucent cards. Keep artistic palette, texture density, shadows, lighting and pixel sampling consistent. Actual authored raster textures, not SVG or primitive final placeholders. Low-resolution render target with nearest upscale; crisp separate HUD. Directional sun and occlusion, real shadow casters; near-field local lamps and emission. Adjustable render resolution and LOD. Original legacy art is candidate source only; no baked 240x240 whole-yard atlas as world authority. Author independent assets with silhouettes, wear, material details, consistent size, animations and collision bounds. Document asset provenance and inspect all angles including low-elevation views.

## World and entity model
Stable IDs, asset ID, transform (position quaternion scale), collision geometry, physical properties, interaction state, parent/attachment graph, inventory ownership, damage, resources. Canonical game state is independent of Three.js objects and Rapier handles. Physics and render are projections of state, not new copies of gameplay objects. Serialize stable IDs and graph, never transient engine handles. Player articulated character, feet-ground contact and movement animation; camera-relative movement, raycast interaction, carrying and placement. Backyard, shed, garage, street, neighbors and junkyard in connected streamed terrain chunks, walkable entrances, no map-selection substitute. Use proximity and line of sight for pickup; physical carrying and inventory storage share entity identity. World state retains removed salvage and placed objects. Collect/return/dismantle/recover parts.

## Construction
In-world workbench and launch pad; orbit camera and close inspection use the same scene. Hybrid sockets and free transforms with placement ghost, rotation, snap, attachment validity, collision and reachable manipulation. Parts store dry mass, fuel volume, density, connection strength, drag area, power draw, thrust direction, thrust curve, efficiency, health and fictional propulsion resources. Validate graph connectivity, tank/engine links, power and controls. Mass properties and center of mass/inertia computed from actual geometry/placement; thrust applied at engine locations. Physically meaningful joints or a compound body for rigid subassemblies; breakable connections via measured loads and explicit failure thresholds, not random breakup. Detach/stage with momentum continuity; debris persists. UI shows real engineering numbers and warns but does not arbitrarily prevent experimental builds. Same assembled graph and visuals through boarding, launch, orbit, landing and recovery. No menu-token ship or replacement silhouette.

## Flight and scale transitions
Canonical inertial Float64 position/velocity/orientation/angular state, plus epoch and reference-frame metadata. Near a surface use local tangent frame with Rapier at fixed 120 Hz target and floating origin; transfer body forces and states consistently. Transform both position and velocity, including rotating body angular velocity cross radius. For long coasts use orbital propagation, not distant rigid bodies. State authority is exclusive per regime (no double integration); frame/solver switch uses the same entity and conserves state, never replaces craft. Hysteresis prevents repeated switches. Camera follows the same entity, changes smoothly with distance, and preserves orientation context. Local terrain stream fades into continuous curvature/atmosphere representation. Planet and distant-body meshes rendered in separate camera-relative depth/scale passes as needed within one renderer, not independent gameplay scenes. A map is an optional view of the same state and trajectory, not a minigame. Return/landing reverses this path. Ground impact and atmospheric heating derived from simulated state.

## Orbital mechanics
Newtonian gravitational acceleration sum over moving bodies, three-dimensional. Use consistent miniature-body radii, gravitational parameters, rotation, atmospheric density functions and orbital separations. Establish stable reference orbits and verify Hill sphere spacing and collisions before freezing parameters. SI internal units. Report altitude, velocity, apoapsis, periapsis, inclination, eccentricity, energy, TWR, available delta-v, maneuver nodes, intercept and time-to-burn. Powered flight includes fuel mass change, thrust, gravity, drag, torque; fictional propellant only. Adaptive RK integration for powered/close encounters, symplectic/coast integration where appropriate, event detection for collision/periapsis/atmosphere entry. Time warp bounded near collisions, burns, and close approaches; never integrate through surfaces. Planner clones current state and uses same physical models as live flight, does not mutate it. Gravity assists emerge from exchange with moving bodies: verify body-relative inbound/outbound hyperbolic excess speed consistency and primary-relative energy change. Planetary transfer, lunar arrival, docking/rendezvous, planetary landing, rovers, satellites, stations, probes and solar escape progression. Assistance may plan burns, not grant success. Numerical reference validation against analytic two-body and independent REBOUND offline tests. No real-world propulsion construction instructions; fictional salvage propulsion, authentic orbital physics.

## Interface, input and audio
One primary viewport throughout. Context-sensitive panels for inventory, part inspector, build tools, flight instrumentation and orbital planner. No modal replacement of world for ordinary actions. Keyboard/mouse, touch and controller mappings; movement, interact, grab/release, rotate/attach, camera, throttle, attitude, stage, warp and map. Context prompts rather than floating labels everywhere. Pause, volume, reduced motion, accessible labels and legible UI. Spatial environment and machinery audio tied to actual objects; wind/exhaust/impact/footsteps driven by events. External sound fades with atmospheric pressure; in vacuum retain appropriate cockpit/contact audio. Save version, entity graph, clock, body states, active craft and progress; atomic saves, migrations, corruption handling and new-game/creative options.

## Acceptance gates (internal; complete-first)
1. Actual WebGL2 + Rapier initialization in supported Chromium and Firefox; verified nonblank frame and real contact test; dependency bundle works without CDN. Clear error on unsupported devices.
2. Walk out of the backyard and back without loading a separate gameplay screen; physically collect and carry a part with collision and persistent identity. Camera rotates fully; no clipping or stack gaps across inspected angles.
3. Attach the same part to the ship in-world; observe changed geometry, mass, inertia, and functional telemetry; remove and drop the same entity. Off-axis thrust produces torque. Collision and staging produce real debris.
4. Board and fly the same assembled craft; no substituted ship asset or hidden restart. Smooth yard/atmosphere/orbit transition. Fuel, stage, health, part IDs and transforms preserved. Return to surface with state continuity.
5. Compare circular orbital period and escape threshold against analytic solutions (<0.1% target relative error in ideal two-body benchmarks). Bound unpowered orbit energy and angular momentum drift (<0.1% target over ten orbits). Check frame conversions round-trip and momentum through staging. Check predicted vs live path with identical controls. Validate actual moving moon flyby, transfer and landing, not just label or scripted animation. Publish measured results and failures.
6. Save during construction and flight, reload and compare IDs, transforms, resources, epoch and state. Test corrupted save recovery and migration. Test touch/controller interactions, audio toggles and accessibility.
7. Visual audit from matching camera views at yard, workshop, flight and space: coherent pixel scale, palette, materials, lighting, no primitive final placeholders, no buried or floating parts, appropriate shadows. Target 60 FPS desktop and mobile with scalable settings; report measured hardware/browser and frame-time percentiles, not assumed device performance.
8. Run actual player-driven end-to-end test: scavenge -> attach -> board -> launch -> stable orbit -> transfer/flyby -> land/recover. GitHub Pages deployed URL opens correct version, catalog play link works, no fatal console exceptions. Document remaining features and do not market a partial prototype as full scope.

## Decisions and limitations
Three.js hybrid sprite-stacks and pixel-textured meshes selected over sprite-stacking every planet literally; physical scale separation and multiple render passes are intentional technical decisions, not separate games. Exact miniature solar system values, asset catalog and performance budgets require implementation calibration. This revision is a specification, not runtime proof. No implementation or deployment claim is made by this document.
